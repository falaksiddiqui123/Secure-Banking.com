INTRODUCTION

Why Online Banking Safety Matters?

Online banking allows people to transfer money, pay bills, check balances and manage their accounts from anywhere.

However, cybercriminals may try to steal personal information through fake messages, websites, applications and phone calls.

Understanding these risks is the first step toward safer digital banking.

01 — RISKS

Common Online Banking Risks

Understand the dangers before they become a problem.

Phishing

Fraudsters may send fake emails or messages containing links designed to steal personal information.

Unsafe Wi-Fi

Using unsecured public networks for sensitive banking activities can expose your information.

Malware

Malicious software can compromise devices and potentially expose sensitive information.

Weak Passwords

Easy-to-guess or reused passwords can make accounts easier to compromise.

02 — FRAUDS

Common Banking Frauds

Recognize suspicious situations and avoid falling for scams.
01

OTP Scams

Someone may pretend to be a bank employee and ask you to reveal an OTP or other confidential information.

02

Fake Customer Care

Scammers may impersonate customer-support representatives and request sensitive information.

03

QR Code Scams

Fraudsters may use misleading QR codes or payment requests to trick people into authorizing transactions.

04

Fake Websites

Fraudulent websites may imitate legitimate banking websites to trick users into entering information.

05

Fake Offers

Fake prizes, loans or investment offers can be used to persuade people to share information or make payments.

06

Fake Apps

Unofficial applications may attempt to obtain unnecessary permissions or sensitive information.

03 — PRECAUTIONS

How to Stay Safe

Follow these simple practices for safer online banking.

Never Share OTP or PIN

Keep OTPs, PINs and passwords private.

Use Strong Passwords

Use strong, unique passwords for important accounts.

Verify Links

Check website addresses before entering sensitive information.

Use Official Apps

Download banking applications from trusted official sources.

Enable Extra Security

Use available multi-factor authentication and security alerts.

Keep Devices Updated

Install security and software updates regularly.

Remember

Your bank will not need your confidential authentication information simply because someone calls or messages you.

Stay Alert

Think You've Encountered Fraud?

Stop the interaction, avoid sharing additional information, and contact your bank through its official communication channels as soon as possible.
